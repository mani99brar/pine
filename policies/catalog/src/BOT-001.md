# BOT-001 Automation and Keeper Reliability — policy version 0.1.0 (DRAFT)

Status: draft for development and staging. Not approved market terms. A version approved for production is published as a new file with a new digest; this text never changes.

## Intended use
Verify one invariant of a keeper, treasury bot, scheduler or other stateful automation system at a pinned commit. Candidate invariant classes: separation of protected fund or accounting categories; no duplicate operations during specified recovery scenarios; safe nonce allocation under specified concurrency; adherence to route, recipient, spender or spending limits; defined behaviour when required inputs become stale or unavailable.

## Claim parameters this policy requires
The claim document must state, in the named fields:
- `claim.requirement`: the one selected invariant, and `claim.policyParameters.sourceRequirement`: where it comes from (document and section).
- `claim.violation`: what resulting plan, state transition or accounting entry demonstrates a violation.
- `claim.policyParameters.startingStates`: the permitted starting states, and how a failing state must be shown to be reachable.
- `claim.scope`: the accounts, chains, routes and accounting categories in scope.
- `claim.faultModel`: the explicit fault model (for example process crash, timeout, replacement transaction). Arbitrary database corruption is excluded unless stated.
- `claim.policyParameters.simulatedAdapters`: which adapters may be simulated, and which conclusions require real integration validation.

## Evidence
A reproducible sequence with initial state, allowed events and faults, the resulting plans and state transitions, and the violation; relevant non-secret journal entries and proposed transaction fields when necessary. For financial invariants, the evidence must distinguish deposit or transfer principal from legitimate transaction-gas expenditure. Shared physical custody of funds alone does not prove an accounting cross-subsidy.

## Exclusions and boundaries
- No claim requires or authorises live transfers, production keys or attacks on services.
- An artificial state that is not reachable under the claim's model is not a counterexample unless the claim explicitly allows initialising it.
- Simulation or fault-injection evidence cannot establish that a real bridge, swap, price feed or deployment integration works.
- A process-level reliability result is not proof of unattended production readiness.

## Outcome
Yes requires a timely, reproducible violation of the selected invariant (rules C3–C5), not an unrelated improvement suggestion. No means none was submitted within scope.
