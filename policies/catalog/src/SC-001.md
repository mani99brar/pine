# SC-001 Smart-Contract Invariant and Security Verification — policy version 0.1.0 (DRAFT)

Status: draft. DISABLED for publication until an approved disclosure process exists (see "Publication gate"). Not approved market terms. This text never changes.

## Intended use
Challenge one particular security property of pinned smart-contract source code or of a precisely specified deployment state. Examples: an unauthorised caller cannot transfer protected assets through identified entry points; a specified accounting invariant holds over an allowed sequence of transactions; a defined signature cannot be replayed under the published domain and nonce assumptions. This is not a full audit or a guarantee of zero vulnerabilities.

## Claim parameters this policy requires
The claim document must state, in the named fields:
- `claim.requirement`: the exact prohibited effect or invariant; if a severity threshold is used, its definitions and exclusions.
- `claim.policyParameters.build`: source and bytecode references and compiler/build settings.
- `claim.policyParameters.deployment`: for state-dependent claims, chain, addresses, block or state snapshot, configuration and proxy implementation references.
- `claim.policyParameters.threatModel`: attacker permissions, initial assets, trusted roles and external dependency assumptions.
- `claim.policyParameters.disclosure`: the approved evidence and disclosure procedure.

## Evidence
A local test or isolated fork reproduction demonstrating the prohibited effect, with setup, transaction sequence, assertions and impact explanation. Simulated or fork execution must not broadcast live transactions.

## Exclusions and boundaries
- Possession of a trusted administrator key unless that capability is expressly within the threat model.
- Profit or damage assumptions that cannot occur under the pinned state and allowed actions.
- Speculative findings without a demonstrated qualifying violation.
- Live exploitation, unauthorised access, or moving other parties' assets.
- Deployment-readiness or real-route claims inferred solely from local tests.

## Publication gate
Claims that may expose live vulnerabilities must not be published until evidence access, responsible disclosure and the adjudicator's ability to examine evidence have been resolved and approved. Keeping exploit evidence confidential while retaining a credible open market is an unresolved design requirement, not an assumed feature.

## Outcome
Yes requires a timely, qualifying demonstration of the defined security violation (rules C3–C5). No means no such evidence was submitted under these rules, not that the contract is safe.
