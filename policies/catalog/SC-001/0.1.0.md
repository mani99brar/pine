# SC-001 Smart-Contract Invariant and Security Verification — policy version 0.1.0 (DRAFT)

Status: draft. DISABLED for publication until an approved disclosure process exists (see "Publication gate"). Not approved market terms. This text never changes.

## Intended use
Challenge one particular security property of pinned smart-contract source code or of a precisely specified deployment state. Examples: an unauthorised caller cannot transfer protected assets through identified entry points; a specified accounting invariant holds over an allowed sequence of transactions; a defined signature cannot be replayed under the published domain and nonce assumptions. This is not a full audit or a guarantee of zero vulnerabilities.

## Claim parameters this policy requires
The claim document must state, in the named fields:
- `claim.requirement`: the exact prohibited effect or invariant; if a severity threshold is used, its definitions and exclusions.
- `claim.policyParameters.build`: source and bytecode references and compiler/build settings.
- `claim.policyParameters.deployment`: for state-dependent claims, chain, addresses, block or state snapshot, configuration and proxy implementation references.
- `claim.policyParameters.threatModel`: attacker permissions, initial assets, trusted roles and external dependency assumptions.
- `claim.policyParameters.disclosure`: the approved evidence and disclosure procedure.

## Evidence
A local test or isolated fork reproduction demonstrating the prohibited effect, with setup, transaction sequence, assertions and impact explanation. Simulated or fork execution must not broadcast live transactions.

## Exclusions and boundaries
- Possession of a trusted administrator key unless that capability is expressly within the threat model.
- Profit or damage assumptions that cannot occur under the pinned state and allowed actions.
- Speculative findings without a demonstrated qualifying violation.
- Live exploitation, unauthorised access, or moving other parties' assets.
- Deployment-readiness or real-route claims inferred solely from local tests.

## Publication gate
Claims that may expose live vulnerabilities must not be published until evidence access, responsible disclosure and the adjudicator's ability to examine evidence have been resolved and approved. Keeping exploit evidence confidential while retaining a credible open market is an unresolved design requirement, not an assumed feature.

## Outcome
Yes requires a timely, qualifying demonstration of the defined security violation (rules C3–C5). No means no such evidence was submitted under these rules, not that the contract is safe.

## Common rules (apply to every claim under this policy)

### C1. What the market asks
The market asks one bounded question: whether a reproducible counterexample demonstrating the specific violation named in the claim document was submitted through the evidence mechanism named in the claim document before its evidence deadline. It does not commission a review, certify the software, or promise any payment to evidence submitters. Only the market's native payout rules govern traders.

### C2. Documents and integrity
- The claim document is identified by the SHA-256 digest of its exact bytes (RFC 8785 canonical JSON). The digest appears in the market question and in the ClaimRegistry record on Gnosis Chain (chain id 100).
- This policy text is identified by the SHA-256 digest of its exact bytes, recorded in the claim document and the ClaimRegistry record.
- Evidence manifests are identified by the SHA-256 digest recorded on-chain at reveal or publication.
- A copy of any of these documents obtained from any source is authentic if and only if its digest matches. Locators (IPFS CIDs, HTTPS mirrors) are conveniences, not authorities.
- If no copy of the claim document matching its recorded digest can be obtained by the person answering or arbitrating, the question cannot be evaluated and the correct answer is Invalid.

### C3. Timeliness
A submission is timely if and only if one of the following holds, judged by Gnosis Chain block timestamps:
1. it was committed with `commitEvidence` on the EvidenceRegistry named in the claim document at a block timestamp strictly earlier than the evidence deadline, and revealed with `revealEvidence` by the same submitter at a block timestamp strictly earlier than the reveal deadline; or
2. it was published with `publishEvidence` on that EvidenceRegistry at a block timestamp strictly earlier than the evidence deadline.

Material posted anywhere else (issues, pull requests, chat, social media, Kleros evidence, the Pine website) is not a submission, although it may be cited as an argument about a timely submission. A reveal recorded at or after the reveal deadline does not make an earlier commitment timely.

### C4. Admissibility
A timely submission is admissible only if:
1. its manifest (and every artifact needed to reproduce it) can be obtained and matches the recorded digests;
2. it identifies this claim (market address, claim document digest and target commit);
3. it gives a reproducible demonstration — setup, environment, exact commands, initial state and the observed versus expected behaviour — that an independent person can follow without secrets, production keys, privileged access or any action against a live system;
4. it demonstrates the specific violation named in the claim document, against the pinned commit, under the claim's stated scope, configuration, environment, allowed inputs, assumptions and fault model.

Not admissible: opinions on style or quality; violations of requirements other than the named one; failures that need a modified target implementation; unsupported or excluded environments and configurations; privileges or initial states the claim does not allow; screenshots, recordings or logs without a reproducible behavioural failure; evidence that depends on attacking deployed systems or moving other parties' assets.

### C5. Correct answer
- **Yes** (answer index 0): at least one timely, admissible submission demonstrates the named violation.
- **No** (answer index 1): no timely, admissible submission demonstrates it.
- **Invalid** (Reality.eth invalid answer): the question cannot be evaluated under C2, or the claim document contradicts itself so that no answer can be determined.

Discoveries submitted after the deadlines do not change the answer; they can support a new claim against a new commit or with new deadlines. The creator of the claim cannot change its requirements, scope or deadlines, and cannot exclude a timely admissible submission.

### C6. Adjudication
Reality.eth answerers and the Kleros arbitrator configured by the Seer market apply this policy, the claim document and the submissions. Reproduction is the adjudicator's responsibility; tooling or summaries provided by Pine or by the claim creator are not authoritative. Technical validity is settled only through this oracle and arbitration process.

### C7. What outcomes do not mean
- "No" means that no qualifying counterexample was submitted in time. It is not a certification that the software is correct, safe or complete.
- A market price reflects the defined submission-and-resolution event, not the probability that the software has no defects.
- Liquidity is not a bounty: it is not reserved for researchers, can be traded against by anyone, and may be withdrawable by whoever provided it.
- Invalid is not a refund. In Seer categorical markets an invalid result pays only the Invalid outcome token.
- Evidence deadlines are not trading cutoffs; outcome tokens may remain transferable and tradable after them.

### C8. Disclosure and conduct
Evidence becomes public when revealed or published. Submitters must not include secrets, credentials, personal data of third parties, or material that enables attacks on deployed systems. Opening a market authorises nobody to test, attack or access any deployed system or real funds.
